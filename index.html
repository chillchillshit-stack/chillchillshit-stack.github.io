With Krassen Krastev · Arsenic, Lausanne.

01 / LOOKING BACK — EVERYDAY ENCOUNTERS

The street enters
the image.

A shared bicycle, a subway ride, a rule that gets in the way. Learning C4D made it possible to build moving images without a film crew. The work took shape through mixing, trying and finding something unexpected.

IN THE WORK / ASIAN SHARING HEAVEN II

The bicycle leaves its daily function. Repeated and recomposed in 3D, it becomes part of an excessive synthetic world.

A SUBWAY RIDE / A SYNTHETIC BODY

The artist’s 3D body enters filmed Shanghai subway spaces. The joke comes from the mismatch between an ordinary setting and an impossible action.

A SMALL IRRITATION / AN ABSURD RESPONSE

Restrictions on carrying passengers on electric bikes in Shanghai become an absurd visual complaint. Humour is a way of working through an everyday frustration.

02 / FROM FILM TO LIVE SYSTEM

From a short film
to a running system.

A BRAVE NEW WORLD is not simply stretched into a longer film. The completed short becomes one source inside a live collaboration. BRAIN IN A VAT then preserves the output of that running system.

01 / RENDERED IN ADVANCE

A finished image sequence. In the later live work, this same short can be switched into the mix as a pre-recorded source.

02 / CHANGING DURING THE LIVE

Alex Wang’s MIDI and audio signals affect game-engine parameters. Predominantly real-time imagery and pre-recorded clips are switched together during the performance.

03 / PRESERVING THE OUTPUT

Almost the entire mixed computer output is recorded—not a camera view of the stage. Light editing, voiceover and subtitles turn the run into the public film.

Room for the unplanned.
But at what cost?

Real-time work could produce relations between sound and image that CHILLCHILLSHIT could not fully predict. This attraction to the unforeseen was different from gaining more precise control.

But a club’s expectations of intensity, dancing and sustained duration could also pull the work away from its initial concerns. Keeping the real-time process does not mean every work has to remain a stage performance. A film can preserve what happened.

A REAL OBJECT / A ROLE REVERSAL

Completed audiovisual performance.

An empty testing booth.
A hand that controls a world.

After mass testing had ended, the booths remained on the street. CHILLCHILLSHIT photographed one and rebuilt it in a game engine, imagining himself inside: instead of being tested, he could extend a gloved hand to test others.

In the realised performance, his hand and a giant virtual hand move together. The virtual hand can push and flick falling human models. A small urban remainder becomes a situation with consequences inside the game world.

Other people have tried the hand interface in informal tests. Those tests are separate from both the completed artist-operated performance and the unrealised gallery proposal.

04 / A QUESTION IN THE PRESENT

What happens
between the parts?

In CONTROL THE WORLD, moving a hand changes what happens to virtual bodies. That is one clear relation. The current question is how several active parts might coexist, rather than all following the same command.

REAL-TIME AUDIOVISUAL PERFORMANCE

Sound synthesis, ComfyUI imagery and TouchDesigner processing are brought into a live performance. The question of how these layers influence one another is still being developed.

PERFORMANCE / 2026

With Krassen Krastev: a performer, projected figures and shadows occupy the same room. The live body is no longer only represented inside a film.

HUMAN SEQUENCER / ONGOING RESEARCH

More than one
leading voice?

Moving crowds, waiting and repetition become material for a developing image-and-sound system. Instead of making every layer follow a single rhythm, the research asks what happens when they can diverge.

“Polyphonic Audiovisual System” is the current working name for this question—not an achieved result, or a theory that guided the early pieces. Earlier experiments have only become readable in this way through looking back.
